Privacy policy — Android app

App: com.focus.android version 1.0.0 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Short version What is stored Usage access Overlay Background What it never does Your control Contact

The short version

FOCUS is a focus timer and app blocker for Android. It has no account, no server, and no internet permission. Nothing you do in FOCUS is sent anywhere, because the app cannot send anything.

What FOCUS stores

On your phone, in the app’s private storage:

  • your blocklist — the package names of the apps you chose to block;
  • your settings: session length, Reels Guard on or off, five-minute passes on or off;
  • today’s focused time and interruption count, your last completed day, and your current streak;
  • whether you have finished onboarding.

This is written with AndroidX DataStore and is readable only by FOCUS. It is not backed up: the app sets android:allowBackup="false", so it is not copied to Google Drive and does not follow you to a new phone.

Usage access

FOCUS asks for Usage access so it can tell which app is in the foreground. It reads only recent activity-resumed events, in a rolling window of a few seconds, and compares the package name against your blocklist. Usage events are not written to disk, not aggregated into history, and not shared.

Without Usage access, FOCUS cannot detect a blocked app, and blocking is off. The app says so instead of pretending to work.

Display over other apps

FOCUS asks for Display over other apps so it can draw its pause screen on top of an app you chose to block. The overlay shows only the blocked app’s name, the session countdown and the two buttons. It is not used to read, capture or record anything on screen, and it never appears over an app that is not on your blocklist.

Notifications and running in the background

FOCUS runs a foreground service with a persistent notification while a session or Reels Guard is active. Android shows that notification; it contains the session status and an “End session” action. FOCUS requests the notification permission on Android 13 and newer so that notification can be displayed.

FOCUS asks to be exempt from battery optimization only through the standard Android settings screen. That keeps the monitor alive while the screen is off. After a reboot, FOCUS restarts its monitor if a session or Reels Guard was active when the phone shut down.

What FOCUS never does

  • No internet permission is declared, so FOCUS cannot transmit data.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking SDKs are included.
  • No account, email address, phone number or identifier is collected.
  • No accessibility service is used, so FOCUS cannot read screen content or simulate taps.
  • No VPN is used, so FOCUS cannot see your traffic.
  • FOCUS does not request QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES; it lists launchable apps through the standard launcher intent query.
  • No data is sold, rented or shared with third parties.

Your control and deletion

  • Remove an app from the blocklist at any time.
  • Turn Reels Guard off, or stop the monitor from its notification.
  • Uninstalling FOCUS deletes all of its data from your phone. There is no copy elsewhere to delete.

Children

FOCUS is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone, of any age.

Other FOCUS products

This policy covers the Android app only. The browser extension has its own policy.

Changes to this policy

If a future version changes how data is handled, this page and the Play Store data safety section will be updated in the same release, before that version ships.
