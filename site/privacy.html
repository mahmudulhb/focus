Privacy policy — browser extension

Applies to the FOCUS browser extension, version 0.4.0 · Last updated 20 November 2026

Short version What is stored What is processed Permissions Reels consent What it never does Your control Contact

The short version

FOCUS has no account, no server and no analytics. Everything it knows is stored in your own browser profile and is never transmitted anywhere. The extension makes no network requests of its own.

What FOCUS stores

Using chrome.storage.local, in your browser profile only:

  • the filtering mode you picked (Basic, Optimal or Complete);
  • the reels switches per platform, and whether you approved the reels blocker at all;
  • the betting-site switch;
  • the list of sites you paused;
  • a daily count of interrupted reel sessions, kept for 60 days and then discarded.

This is not chrome.storage.sync: it is not copied to your Google account, and it is not shared with the developer or anyone else.

What FOCUS processes while you browse

  • Request URLs, by the browser's declarative rules engine, to decide whether a request matches the packaged ad, tracker or betting rules. FOCUS does not read request or response bodies, headers or cookies, and keeps no log of requests.
  • The hostname of the active tab, when you open the popup, so it can show whether FOCUS is active or paused on that site.
  • The URL path of the current page, on the five reels platforms, to notice a reel route. The path is compared in memory; it is not written to storage.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • storage — saves the settings listed above in your browser profile.
  • declarativeNetRequest — applies the packaged filter rules. Your browser enforces them; the extension never inspects traffic itself.
  • tabs — reads the active tab's hostname for the popup's site control, and opens the extension's own permission page after install.
  • Content scripts — a stylesheet on any site to hide ad containers in Complete mode, and the reels blocker on instagram.com, facebook.com, youtube.com, tiktok.com and snapchat.com only. No host_permissions are requested.

FOCUS does not request webRequest, cookies, history, scripting, downloads, clipboard or identity.

The reels blocker is opt-in

The reels blocker changes what a page shows, so it does nothing until you approve it on the permission screen that opens after install. That screen lists the five sites and what happens on each. Consent is never inferred from an older setting, so an extension update cannot switch the blocker back on for someone who never approved it. Turning it off in the popup or settings withdraws it immediately.

What FOCUS never does

  • It does not collect or transmit personal data — there is nowhere to transmit it.
  • It does not use remote code. All JavaScript ships in the package.
  • It does not read page content, form fields, messages or cookies.
  • It does not collect identifiers, location, health, financial or authentication information.
  • It does not sell or transfer data to third parties.
  • It does not use data to determine creditworthiness or for lending.
  • It does not inject advertising, and the extension itself contains no ads.

Your control and deletion

  • Pause any site from the popup; the allowlist always outranks the filters.
  • Switch off protection entirely with the master switch.
  • Remove the extension to delete everything it stored. There is no copy elsewhere to delete, because none was ever made.

Children

FOCUS is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone, of any age.

Other FOCUS products

This policy covers the browser extension. The FOCUS app for Android is a separate product with its own policy and its own on-device storage.

Changes to this policy

If a future version starts collecting anything, this page and the Chrome Web Store privacy disclosure will be updated in the same release, before that version ships. The effective date at the top always reflects the published version.
